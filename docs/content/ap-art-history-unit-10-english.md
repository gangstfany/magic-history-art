## AP #224: The Gates

### Identification

- Maker: Christo and Jeanne-Claude.
- Cultural/artistic context: Site-specific contemporary public art.
- Date: 1979–2005 C.E.
- Medium: Mixed-media installation.
- Type: Temporary landscape installation.
- Source geography: Central Park, New York City, United States.
- Learning group: Public Space & Memory.

The anchor represents the actual original architectural or public-art site.

### Function

A temporary pedestrian experience reframed familiar park routes and invited public participation without commemorating a single historical event.

### Form

Saffron-colored rectangular frames and hanging fabric followed existing paths. Wind, winter light and moving pedestrians continually changed the appearance of the repeated structures.

### Content

The gates proposed thresholds without one privileged entrance or viewpoint. Visitors passed beneath fabric canopies, making the designed landscape and bodily movement part of the work.

### Context

Conceived in 1979 and realized briefly in 2005, the project was financed through the artists' preparatory work rather than admission charges. Central Park is a designed landscape, not untouched wilderness.

### Recognition Anchors

- saffron fabric.
- repeated rectangular gates.
- winter park paths.

### Comparisons

AP #225, Vietnam Veterans Memorial (`ap225-vietnam-veterans-memorial`): Both depend on walking visitors; temporary colored gates animate public space, while the permanent inscribed wall organizes mourning.

## AP #225: Vietnam Veterans Memorial

### Identification

- Maker: Maya Lin.
- Cultural/artistic context: Contemporary American commemorative design.
- Date: 1982 C.E.
- Medium: Granite.
- Type: Public memorial.
- Source geography: National Mall, Washington, D.C., United States.
- Learning group: Public Space & Memory.

The anchor represents the actual original architectural or public-art site.

### Function

The memorial provides a place for individual mourning and collective remembrance of the dead and missing rather than narrating the war through victorious heroic figures.

### Form

Two polished black granite arms form a V cut into the earth. Descending and ascending paths shape the encounter; reflective surfaces place visitors' images beside the incised names.

### Content

Names arranged by the chronology of loss return to the center where the sequence begins and ends. Particular names give the abstract geometry an intimate human presence.

### Context

Lin's anonymous competition design encountered racist hostility and criticism of abstraction after her identity became known. Later nearby figurative additions should not be confused with her original wall design.

### Recognition Anchors

- V-shaped recessed wall.
- polished black granite.
- dense inscribed names.

### Comparisons

AP #224, The Gates (`ap224-gates`): Both make walking a mode of viewing; the memorial descends into the earth and focuses on names, while the gates spread a temporary experience along park paths.

## AP #226: Horn Players

### Identification

- Maker: Jean-Michel Basquiat.
- Cultural/artistic context: African American and diasporic contemporary painting.
- Date: 1983 C.E.
- Medium: Acrylic and oil paintstick on three canvas panels.
- Type: Triptych painting.
- Source geography: United States (Basquiat's New York painting practice).
- Learning group: Identity & Representation.

The artist's working context is used; the CED does not identify the precise studio. Los Angeles is the current collection, not the creation site.

### Function

The painting honors Black jazz innovators and gives their creativity prominence through an intentionally layered visual and verbal tribute.

### Form

Three dark panels carry schematic figures, white painted interruptions and repeated or crossed-out words. Uneven marks and abrupt shifts generate a visual rhythm comparable to musical improvisation.

### Content

Charlie Parker and Dizzy Gillespie appear through figures, instruments and verbal references. Ornithology invokes Parker's nickname Bird and his composition, making language part of the portrait.

### Context

Basquiat brought street-based wordplay, Black cultural history and art-historical references into painting. Apparent disorder is organized through repetition; the triptych is one work and one official required image.

### Recognition Anchors

- black triptych.
- crossed-out words.
- saxophone and jazz names.

### Comparisons

AP #238, Electronic Superhighway (`ap238-electronic-superhighway`): Both layer and repeat information; Basquiat focuses on jazz figures, whereas Paik assembles a national media landscape from screens.

## AP #227: Summer Trees

### Identification

- Maker: Song Su-nam.
- Cultural/artistic context: Korean modern ink painting; Sumukhwa.
- Date: 1979 C.E.
- Medium: Ink on paper.
- Type: Abstract ink landscape.
- Source geography: South Korea (modern ink-painting context).
- Learning group: Abstraction & Landscape.

Korean production tradition is established; an exact studio is not established by the CED. London is the current holding.

### Function

Modern ink practice renews literati values while addressing Korean cultural identity; the work is not merely an imitation of Western abstract painting.

### Form

Broad vertical strokes range from saturated black to diluted gray. Wet-paper bleeding and drier marks coexist, while narrow white intervals create a compressed suggestion of space.

### Content

The title directs the abstract bands toward trees and nature. Friendship and upright character are possible literati associations, not an established exclusive iconographic code.

### Context

The 1979 painting precedes the organized ink movement of the 1980s. Postwar division and Westernizing pressures made the choice of ink and paper culturally meaningful in South Korea.

### Recognition Anchors

- broad vertical ink bands.
- black-to-gray washes.
- white gaps.

### Comparisons

AP #234, Earth's Creation (`ap234-earths-creation`): Both ground abstraction in particular cultural relationships to landscape; restrained ink tones differ from Kngwarreye's dense, multicolored touches.

## AP #228: Androgyne III

### Identification

- Maker: Magdalena Abakanowicz.
- Cultural/artistic context: Polish postwar fiber sculpture.
- Date: 1985 C.E.
- Medium: Burlap, resin, wood, nails, and string.
- Type: Hollow figurative sculpture.
- Source geography: Poland (Abakanowicz's working context).
- Learning group: Body & Spirituality.

The supplied chapter establishes the artist's continuing Polish practice; the precise fabrication workshop is not identified. New York is the current holding.

### Function

The anonymous fragment invites reflection on vulnerability, war and dehumanization while refusing to make gender the figure's defining identity.

### Form

Resin-stiffened burlap forms a hollow headless torso. Wrinkled surfaces suggest skin and muscle, while a rough wooden support supplies a structural base rather than anatomically modeled legs.

### Content

Absent limbs and head, the empty cavity and textured shell make the body simultaneously present and deprived. Reduced sexual characteristics connect to the title's androgyny.

### Context

The artist's wartime experiences and postwar Polish restrictions inform readings of trauma. Its ambiguity remains important: it is not a documented portrait of one particular victim.

### Recognition Anchors

- headless burlap torso.
- wrinkled hollow shell.
- rough wooden stretcher.

### Comparisons

AP #242, Lying with the Wolf (`ap242-lying-wolf`): Both use nonidealized bodies to explore vulnerability; the isolated bodily husk contrasts with the woman and wolf's intimate mutual care.

### Required Views and Sources

1. `figure-and-wooden-support`: Headless burlap-resin torso seated on its wooden support. [College Board CED, physical p. 294 / printed p. 287](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-art-history-course-and-exam-description.pdf).

## AP #229: A Book from the Sky

### Identification

- Maker: Xu Bing.
- Cultural/artistic context: Chinese contemporary printmaking and language-based installation.
- Date: 1987–1991 C.E.
- Medium: Mixed-media installation.
- Type: Printed-book and scroll installation.
- Source geography: Beijing, China (initial 1988 presentation).
- Learning group: Language & Media.

The initial completed section was shown at the National Art Museum of China in 1988; the finished installation was first shown at Tokyo Gallery in 1991. This anchor records the initial presentation, not every fabrication site or later installation.

### Function

An unreadable text confronts even literate readers with uncertainty and questions the authority of language, knowledge and printed information.

### Form

Invented Chinese-looking characters were carved as wooden type and printed into books and scrolls. Floor, walls and ceiling envelop visitors in a highly ordered textual environment.

### Content

The characters borrow the visual structure of Chinese writing without ordinary lexical meaning. The dignity of traditional books conflicts with the absence of readable content.

### Context

Post-Mao intellectual exchange and the artist's earlier experience of propaganda lettering supply context. An initial section appeared in Beijing in 1988; the finished form appeared at Tokyo Gallery in 1991.

### Recognition Anchors

- books across floor.
- billowing ceiling scrolls.
- unreadable pseudo-characters.

### Comparisons

AP #250, Kui Hua Zi (Sunflower Seeds) (`ap250-sunflower-seeds`): Both use repetition and labor to challenge apparent uniformity; the book repeats unreadable signs, while the seeds preserve individual differences within a seemingly identical mass.

### Required Views and Sources

## AP #230: Pink Panther

### Identification

- Maker: Jeff Koons.
- Cultural/artistic context: Postmodern mass-culture appropriation; European artisan fabrication.
- Date: 1988 C.E.
- Medium: Glazed porcelain.
- Type: Porcelain figurative sculpture.
- Source geography: Europe (artisan fabrication; workshop unresolved).
- Learning group: Materials & Global Exchange.

MoMA identifies European artisan fabrication for the Banality series. A country or workshop for this edition is not established here; New York is an original 1988 exhibition context, not assumed fabrication.

### Function

The sculpture elevates celebrity imagery and toy-like forms into a luxury art object, provoking debate over the boundary between cultivated taste and kitsch.

### Form

Glossy glaze, sugary pastel colors and enlarged figurine scale produce a meticulously finished yet deliberately excessive object.

### Content

A blonde pin-up modeled on Jayne Mansfield embraces the Pink Panther. Sexualized celebrity and cartoon consumption overlap in a deliberately artificial pairing.

### Context

Part of the 1988 Banality series, the design was fabricated by European artisans. Evidence reviewed does not establish this edition's country or workshop; its New York exhibition context is not a fabrication location.

### Recognition Anchors

- blonde pin-up.
- pink cartoon panther.
- glossy pastel porcelain.

### Comparisons

AP #250, Kui Hua Zi (Sunflower Seeds) (`ap250-sunflower-seeds`): Both depend on artisan porcelain fabrication and altered everyday scale; Koons magnifies a figurine into luxury art, while Ai's vast field examines the individual and the collective.

## AP #233: Trade (Gifts for Trading Land with White People)

### Identification

- Maker: Jaune Quick-to-See Smith.
- Cultural/artistic context: Salish and Kootenai contemporary art; Indigenous critique.
- Date: 1992 C.E.
- Medium: Oil and mixed media on canvas.
- Type: Mixed-media triptych and suspended objects.
- Source geography: United States (Quick-to-See Smith's artistic practice).
- Learning group: Materials & Global Exchange.

The artist's Salish Kootenai affiliation and Montana origin are not evidence of this work's precise studio; the current Norfolk collection is not the production site.

### Function

The work ironically offers stereotyped souvenirs in return for land, exposing colonial dispossession and commodified Indigenous identities.

### Form

Newspaper and dripping paint layer across three canvases. A canoe outline spans the painting, while toys and souvenirs hang on a line above it.

### Content

The canoe evokes travel and exchange. Commercial mascots and souvenirs provide evidence of stereotyping and serve as sarcastic bargaining objects.

### Context

Created for the Columbus quincentenary, the painting contests celebratory colonial history. The cheap-purchase story about Manhattan is treated as an ideological myth, not secure historical fact.

### Comparisons

AP #237, Pisupo Lua Afe (Corned Beef 2000) (`ap237-pisupo-lua-afe`): Both use consumer objects to critique colonial economies; Trade emphasizes land and stereotypes, while Tuffery addresses food sovereignty and environment.
