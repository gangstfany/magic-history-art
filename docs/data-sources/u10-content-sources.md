## Primary corroboration and limits

- AP229: [Xu Bing's making account](https://www.xubing.com/en/database/writing/289) corroborates multi-stage fabrication and first encounter with the finished work in Tokyo; [artist chronology](https://www.xubing.com/cn/about/workhistory) is already recorded in the frozen ledger for Beijing1988 and Tokyo1991. No single studio is inferred from the whole chronology.
- AP230: current indexed [MoMA object record](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/81095/) confirms the Banality series, European artisans, Jayne Mansfield imagery and New York1988 exhibition. Direct open timed out, but indexed primary text was available. Country/workshop remains unresolved; the supplied guide's Germany/Italy statement applies to series manufacture and is not edition-specific evidence.
- AP239: [SCAD Museum of Art](https://www.scadmoa.org/exhibitions/2011/the-crossing) directly confirms SCAD's1996 co-commission and university premiere. Its2011–2012 display is later, not the first presentation.
- AP245: current [Harn collection overview](https://harn.ufl.edu/collections/contemporary) and [exact object record](https://harn.emuseum.com/objects/10025/old-mans-cloth) corroborate title,2003 date and aluminum/copper medium. The object database lists “Place of origin Ghana,” conflicting with the frozen broad Nsukka working-context anchor. It may encode artist-origin rather than fabrication, but that is an inference, not resolved evidence. Keep Nsukka explicitly qualified; do not claim a documented workshop address or first venue. Supplied guide pp.195–197 describes other hangings rather than this exact object.
- AP244/AP250: direct Tate page access returned403/unavailable during this pass. Context and access-change explanation therefore rest on the supplied guide and frozen ledger; no claim is made that fresh direct Tate reading succeeded. The unrelated indexed Tate mirror was not used as independent authority.
