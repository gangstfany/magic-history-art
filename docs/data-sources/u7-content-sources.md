## AP181 — Petra

Official identification: PDF p. 4 / printed p. 132, three images. Notes: PDF pp. 124–125. Smarthistory volume: PDF pp. 179–189 / printed pp. 167–177.

Evidence: [UNESCO Petra](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/326/) establishes the Nabataean city and rock-cut/constructed architectural heritage. [Petra official visitor authority, Great Temple](https://www.visitpetra.jo/en/Location/110) explicitly notes a likely secular function with possible religious activities. The supplied Smarthistory articles distinguish the rock-cut Treasury from a developed urban center and discuss the Great Temple's audience-hall interpretation.

Decisions: retain the AP c. 400 B.C.E.–100 C.E. collective label; avoid treating that span as a precise Treasury date. Treasury is probably funerary, not proven storage. Great Temple is a conventional name, not certain religious function. Do not repeat the notes' claim that the entire city was carved from a cliff. Views: `petra-context`, `treasury`, `great-temple`.

## AP182 — Buddha, Bamiyan

Official identification: PDF p. 5 / printed p. 133, two images. Notes: PDF p. 125. Smarthistory volume: PDF pp. 191–197 / printed pp. 179–185.

Evidence: [UNESCO Bamiyan](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/208/) identifies the Buddhist cave landscape, cultural interchange, and deliberate destruction of both giant Buddhas in March 2001. [Smarthistory Bamiyan Buddhas](https://smarthistory.org/bamiyan-buddhas/) explains sixth–seventh-century dating, plaster/paint, and circulation around portions of the figures.

Decisions: retain official c. 400–800 range while distinguishing narrower scholarly dating. Exact Buddha identities are interpreted rather than universally demonstrated. Archival intact figures and current empty niches require different captions. Destruction is attributed to the Taliban in 2001, not generalized to Islamic culture. Views: `buddha-front`, `buddha-context`.

## AP183 — The Kaaba

Official identification: PDF pp. 5–6 / printed pp. 133–134, three images. Notes: PDF pp. 125–126. Smarthistory volume: PDF pp. 199–201 / printed pp. 187–189.

Evidence: [Smarthistory, The Kaaba](https://smarthistory.org/the-kaaba/) identifies the sanctuary, kiswah, qibla, hajj, and successive renovations. The supplied article explains its setting inside Masjid al-Haram and the tradition of Ibrahim/Ismail. Official labels establish the broad rededication date, distinct from more detailed historical chronologies.

Decisions: tawaf consists of seven circuits, correcting the notes' five. Use no fragile present-day crowd estimate or current renovation claim. Kaaba is not the entire surrounding mosque and is not worshipped as a god. Foundation traditions remain explicitly traditions. Views: `circumambulation`, `exterior`, `gathering-context`.

## AP184 — Jowo Rinpoche

Official identification: PDF p. 6 / printed p. 134, one image. Notes: PDF pp. 126–127. Smarthistory volume: PDF pp. 203–205 / printed pp. 191–193.

Evidence: [Smarthistory Jowo article](https://smarthistory.org/jowo-rinpoche-jokhang-temple-tibet/) and its supplied textbook version explain the earth-touching gesture, devotional dress, later restorations, and traditional arrival with Wencheng in 641. The book critically distinguishes sacred origin stories from art-historical dating.

Decisions: no assertion of a Buddha-lifetime portrait or securely located casting workshop. Yarlung is a dynasty, not the woman who transported the statue. Lhasa is the historic devotional setting. Later crown and robes do not establish the seventh-century appearance. View: `primary`.

## AP185 — Dome of the Rock

Official identification: PDF p. 6 / printed p. 134, two images. Notes: PDF p. 127. Smarthistory volume: PDF pp. 207–209 / printed pp. 195–197.

Evidence: [Metropolitan Museum, Dome of the Rock](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2012/byzantium-and-islam/blog/where-in-the-world/posts/dome-of-the-rock) distinguishes a shrine from a mosque, notes debates about original purpose, and discusses recombination of Byzantine architectural/mosaic vocabulary. The supplied textbook details the octagon, two ambulatories, central rock, calligraphy, crown motifs, and later exterior treatments.

Decisions: identify Abd al-Malik as patron; preserve 691–692 completion label. Night Journey links are religious traditions. Avoid a claim that all today's tiles or gold covering are seventh-century. Do not frame the original purpose as securely proven. Views: `exterior`, `interior`.

## AP186 — Great Mosque, Isfahan

Official identification: PDF p. 7 / printed p. 135, four images. Notes: PDF pp. 127–128. Smarthistory volume: PDF pp. 211–213 / printed pp. 199–201.

Evidence: [UNESCO Isfahan](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1397/) confirms multi-century building layers, the Seljuk four-iwan transformation, and integration with bazaar fabric. [Smarthistory Great Mosque](https://smarthistory.org/the-great-mosque-or-masjid-e-jameh-of-isfahan/) explains open courtyard, domes, and city connections. [Smarthistory mosque types](https://smarthistory.org/common-types-of-mosque-architecture/) defines an iwan and the qibla-side emphasis.

Decisions: present c. 700 as the AP foundation label, not the construction date of every component. This is not the Shah/Imam Mosque of the Safavid square. Mihrab is a prayer-direction niche, not an altar. The required plaster mihrab must not be replaced with the Met's tile mihrab from a different Isfahan building. Views: `iwan`, `iwan-detail`, `courtyard`, `mihrab`.

## AP187 — Qur'an folio

Official identification: PDF p. 8 / printed p. 136, one image. Notes: PDF pp. 128–129. Smarthistory volume: PDF pp. 215–217 / printed pp. 203–205.

Evidence: [Smarthistory Folio from a Qur'an](https://smarthistory.org/folio-from-a-quran/) identifies the Morgan manuscript MS M.712, dark Kufic, red vowels, chapter ornament, al-‘Ankabut, and a 911 donation associated with Damascus. The article explicitly leaves origin and production time before donation uncertain, with “possibly Iraq” as an attribution.

Decisions: Arabic reads right to left, correcting the notes. Preserve the official eighth–ninth-century range and broad North Africa/Near East production qualifier. A Damascus donation is not proof of manufacture there. Do not substitute any visually similar Kufic or blue Qur'an. View: `primary`.

## AP188 — Basin

Official identification: PDF p. 8 / printed p. 136, one image. Notes: PDF p. 129. Smarthistory volume: PDF pp. 219–221 / printed pp. 207–209.

Evidence: [Louvre LP16](https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl010318774) records brass with engraved gold/silver inlay, signatures, Egypt/Syria marked uncertain, and c. 1325–1340. [Smarthistory Framing Islamic Art](https://smarthistory.org/reframing-art-history/framing-islamic-art/) separates Mamluk manufacture from later French baptisms and rejects an association with Louis IX. The supplied basin conversation discusses horsemen, animals, water creatures, absence of a dominant patron inscription, and disputed iconography.

Decisions: retain AP c. 1320–1340, noting the slightly narrower Louvre range here. Precise initial patron/function and narrative identities are unresolved. Do not assert a documented Mamluk-versus-Mongol battle or original French Christian commission. France is later reuse, not the map's production location. View: `primary`.

## AP189 — Bahram Gur Fights the Karg

Official identification: PDF p. 8 / printed p. 136, one image. Notes: PDF p. 130. Smarthistory volume: PDF pp. 223–229 / printed pp. 211–217.

Evidence: [Harvard Art Museums 1960.190](https://harvardartmuseums.org/collections/object/169542) identifies the exact folio, c. 1335, Ilkhanid Iran/Tabriz, paper with ink/colors/gold/silver, European textile imagery, Chinese landscape conventions, and the assumed court commission. The supplied article explains the Shahnama story, Indian narrative setting, and different Karg translations.

Decisions: preserve AP c. 1330–1340. Tabriz is a court attribution; the catalog's localized label does not turn an assumed specific commission into documentary certainty. Ancient Sasanian hero, fourteenth-century Mongol patronage, and Indian narrative location are separate contexts. No substitution of another Bahram episode. View: `primary`.

## AP190 — Court of Gayumars

Official identification: PDF p. 8 / printed p. 136, one image. Notes: PDF p. 130. Smarthistory volume: PDF pp. 231–235 / printed pp. 219–223.

Evidence: [Aga Khan Museum AKM165](https://remastered.agakhanmuseum.org/themes/the-courageous-spirit/objects/shah-tahmasp-shahnameh/akm165.html) attributes Sultan Muhammad, places the page in Tabriz c. 1524–1525, and explains the ideal court's tragic background concerning Siyamak. [Smarthistory Court of Gayumars](https://smarthistory.org/the-court-of-gayumars/) and supplied volume discuss leopard skins, Safavid patronage, Tabriz/Herat traditions, and collaborative manuscript production.

Decisions: retain official c. 1522–1525 and official materials; museum date is narrower and also records silver. Traditional master attribution does not imply one artist made the whole manuscript. Figural secular epic is not Qur'anic illustration. View: `primary`.

## AP191 — Ardabil Carpet

Official identification: PDF p. 9 / printed p. 137, one image. Notes: PDF p. 130. Smarthistory volume: PDF pp. 237–241 / printed pp. 225–229.

Evidence: [V&A Ardabil Carpet](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/the-ardabil-carpet) describes silk/wool, medallion and unequal lamps, inscription date AH 946 / 1539–1540, Maqsud's probable supervisory role, and shrine association. [Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ardabil-carpet-persian-carpet-acquired-by-the-victoria-and-albert-museum-in-1893/) provides inscription and provenance scholarship. The supplied volume explicitly lists multiple possible weaving cities and discusses repair with the companion carpet.

Decisions: retain regional Safavid Iran anchor. The V&A summary both names Ardabil and acknowledges unclear exact origins; it is not sufficient to prove a workshop there. Maqsud's Kashan designation is likewise not proof of weaving location. Unequal-lamp perspective is a proposed explanation, not demonstrated intent. Do not repeat the supplied textbook's typo dating the Hijra to 620: the historical epoch is 622, and the study prose needs only the verified AH 946 equivalence. Target the complete V&A carpet, not the incomplete LACMA companion. View: `primary`.
