## Adjudications and uncertainty retained

- AP213: naturally prismatic basalt is not a universal claim of hand-carved geometric columns. The broad CED date is distinct from the narrower major monumental-building phase.
- AP214: no specific ahu is named from the required photograph alone. Generic claims that every moai faces away from the sea are not repeated. Red pukao are present in this required view, not mandatory for all moai.
- AP215: the guide's museum essay discusses a related small cape and supports construction and general cultural context. Its dimensions and exact-object transport history are not assigned to the CED cape. Study-note claims identifying Captain Cook's widow or a particular feather count are not adopted without exact-object corroboration. CED late-eighteenth-century dating is retained.
- AP216: Tangaroa identification is speculative, not established; the original phallic lower end is missing. Men and women's material labor and barkcloth enclosure matter. The contextual engraving remains a third mandatory view, not an optional detail.
- AP217: geographic Micronesia does not erase Polynesian cultural and linguistic affiliations. General traditions of named figures, family groups, priests and temples do not supply a verified name or original temple for this object.
- AP218: the illustrated mask is the Met's specific Mabuiag object, not an interchangeable regional example. Ancestor/hero/totemic readings and local ritual use versus manufacture for outsiders remain qualified.
- AP219: tapa is beaten bark, not woven cloth. General clothing, display and exchange functions do not establish this object's original commission. Human figures and writing on some hiapo are not automatically present on this selected textile.
- AP220: 1890 posthumous Lindauer oil painting; Nene died in 1871, not three years before the painting. Likely photographic mediation is not the photograph itself. Goldie's later painting is a different work. Artistic intervention and descendant relationships complicate a purely documentary reading.
- AP221: mnemonic learning on shore, not GPS or routine onboard reference. Related stick charts can have shell markers; the selected CED identification is wood and fiber. No invented precise maker or island.
- AP222: mask first, display second. The selected twentieth-century materials/date follow CED. The guide also illustrates other earlier malagan works for interpretation; they do not replace the selected components. Museum holdings do not prove that the two selected views derive from one shared ceremony.
- AP223: performance in Fiji in 1953, exact photograph date and venue unknown. Mats visibly carried are woven; masi clothing is beaten barkcloth. The photograph does not directly show every textile being handed over or preserve chant, scent and full movement. Study-note confusion between Fiji and Tonga is not repeated.

## Comparison policy

Each work has a specific, substantive comparison to another new U9 ID, all AP≤223. Notes distinguish materials, scale, activation, geography or representational strategy rather than merely saying “same culture.” Relationships are formal/interpretive comparisons, not claims of direct historical influence. AP213↔214 examines monumental chiefly/ancestral authority; AP214↔217 contrasts platform monument and temple image; AP215→216 and AP216→217 compare enclosure, fiber and sacred bodies; AP218↔222 compares composite masks and differing ritual roles; AP219↔223 differentiates painted object from multimedia exchange; AP220→214 examines ancestral presence through different representational media; AP221→213 treats water as a connector at cognitive versus architectural scale.

## Direct 1600×800 world anchors

Coordinates are direct APP WORLD points, not the legacy miniature-map fallback. Existing SITE_WORLD_COORDINATES was inspected: its Asian and older Pacific examples are hand-tuned and do not imply one reliable geographic formula. The actual world SVG's Australian outline provides a useful Pacific-side calibration. These illustrative world anchors use x≈800+(40/9)×longitude and y≈400−(40/9)×latitude, rounded and checked for finite bounds. They are study-navigation approximations, not GIS provenance claims. Dateline separation is intentional: Fiji/Marshall lie toward the right edge, Hawai'i/Cook Islands/Niue toward the left. Parent integration must register these siteName keys or assign worldX/worldY; passing them through the legacy fallback would distort their position.
